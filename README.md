### A Question for Vibe Coders

You are using AI to do your work, and AI outputs long explanations of what it did after doing the work for you… but you don’t read them?

Just reading what the AI wrote will show you some of the holes. Why not do that if you are not even going to read your own code?

We will cover that in this course. If you are not going to even read your own code, at least read what the AI says.

### A Hard Truth

You will not like some of what I say, but here it is: pure vibe coding is not engineering.

Engineering is not a degree. It is not a course. Engineering is a specific way of approaching a problem and a task. It is a method of thinking that abides by a set of first principles and a structured model of cognition used to tackle the task of creating a solution.

Those first principles include:

1. Understand the problem and its constraints before building a solution.
2. Explicitly consider what can go wrong (failure modes, edge cases, adversarial conditions).
3. Make trade-offs conscious and documented rather than accidental.
4. Design so the solution can be verified and observed.
5. Account for the full lifecycle — maintainability, security, and operational reality, not just “it works now.”
6. Validate assumptions with evidence instead of accepting the first thing that appears to work.
7. Maintain ownership and accountability for the outcome.

Sending a prompt into an agent without any due diligence or analysis, letting the agent run, and then not reading the output or what the agent says, and then shipping that as a product (more accurately, that product is called a liability) does not adhere to *any* of those first principles.

Therefore, vibe coding is not engineering. There, I proved it.

To be an engineer, you have to abide by those first principles, and that requires careful attention to detail.
